## What we are building

Two things, in this order, as a wedge into schools:

**1. An LMS for schools.** Teachers set homework, quizzes, tests and
assignments; they see how their students are doing.

**2. A student homework app** connected to it. The student does the work; the
LAM backend tracks their progress across homework, quizzes, tests and
assignments.

**White-label** — a student should experience it as *their own school's* app,
not as a product we sell to their school.

Inside the student app there can be **a text-based AI agent** that helps with
doubts and with working through an assignment. **Text only.** No voice, no
avatar, no live class.

**The full AI tutor comes later, as an update**, with pricing configured. That
is the whole point of this order: get schools onto the LMS, get students using
it daily, and then adding the tutor is an update to an app they already have
rather than a new thing to sell.

## What already exists, so we do not rebuild it

The repo is not empty for this. Migration **0005 is live and was written for
exactly this** — its own comments say `'school' is what the LMS will create`
and that the `teacher` and `admin` roles exist "so the check constraint does not
have to be altered on the day the LMS ships". `organization`, `membership` and
`org_id` on every table that needs one are already deployed.

Roughly 9,000 lines were never about voice: `learner-model` (per-outcome
mastery — this IS progress tracking), `knowledge-graph`, `ledger`, `diagnostic`
(the quiz and test engine), `safety`, `pedagogy`, `syllabus`, and `relay` —
which already holds the only vendor keys behind auth, spend metering, a daily
cap, a kill switch, OTP and consent. **The text agent uses that relay. Do not
grow a second one.**

`tutor-judge` is worth reading before writing any short-answer grading: judging
free text against an authored expectation is what it already does.

Leave the tutor-specific packages alone: `voice`, `board-layout`, `class-prep`,
`stage`, `player`, `action-player`, `persona`, `doubt-session`, `graphics`.

## How I want you to work

**Do not start coding.** The product shape is not defined yet — we have a
direction, not a spec. Brainstorm with me first and ask what you need. Things I
expect you to ask about: how a teacher actually creates and assigns work, what
"white-label" means concretely (one app with per-school branding, or a separate
build per school), what a parent sees if anything, whether marks/grading is ours
or the teacher's, and how a school gets set up the first time.

Answer in Hinglish, briefly. Tell me what is true, including when something we
already believe does not hold up — that has happened repeatedly and it has
always been worth it.

Rules that carry over and are not up for debate:

- **App chrome is always English.** (A tutor's spoken register was a separate
  per-student choice; that question returns when the tutor does.)
- **Users are children.** Reject rather than sanitise, fail closed, and never
  let a comment claim a protection the code does not have. This repo shipped a

- **A comment is a claim** and gets checked like one.
- **The relay is the only holder of vendor keys.**
