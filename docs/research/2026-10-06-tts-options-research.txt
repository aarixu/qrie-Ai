
==================== PHONE-NATIVE ====================
SUMMARY: Best cheap path: the phone's own voice through the browser's speechSynthesis in Chrome on Android (90.6% of Indian mobile browsing, Sept 2026). It costs Rs 0 per minute, against about Rs 3.6 for Gemini today.
Limits: you cannot pick the voice, a phone without a Hindi voice pack silently speaks Hindi in an English voice, and Chrome Android gives onstart/onend but no word-boundary events. I did not find any source showing how a device voice reads Roman-letter Hinglish. That is the biggest unknown.
So send Hindi words in Devanagari and keep English science terms in Latin script. Do not count on Roman Hinglish until someone listens on 3 real phones.
iPhone Safari shows only pre-installed voices and needs a tap. Edge on Windows has the best free voices (Swara and Madhur for Hindi, Neerja and Prabhat for Indian English) but Edge is 8.4% of Indian desktop. Chrome's built-in AI APIs have no text-to-speech.
Suggested design: device voice by default, a one-tap "can you hear Hindi?" check, and Gemini TTS only as fallback. Also worth pricing: Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS, about Rs 0.8 per minute (see caveats).
CAVEATS: NOT VERIFIED: I did not hear any phone voice. I had no phone and no connected real Chrome. My only hands-on probe was a Windows 11 PC; the embedded browser pane listed voices but speak() failed ('synthesis-failed') even for English, so I measured no onstart/onboundary timing. The Windows audio probe (duration, plus a Windows en-US recogniser as a rough ear) is weak evidence and applies only to the old Microsoft Hindi desktop voices. Gaps: (1) How any on-device voice reads ROMAN Hinglish: no developer report found, only vendor marketing claims and inference. ==================== CLOUD-APIS ====================
SUMMARY: 1) No paid cloud TTS with Hindi comes near the INR 5 per student-hour ceiling. The paid options run INR 0.3 to 3.5 per minute of speech, which is INR 19 to 210 per hour of continuous speech. Only the free tiers (Google Standard/WaveNet 4M chars a month, Polly Standard 5M chars a month) and hosted open models (DeepInfra Kokoro INR 0.05/min, Chatterbox INR 0.08/min) get close. Phone-native TTS plus caching is therefore the real cost lever.
2) The cheapest big-vendor step down from today is Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS at USD 6 per 1M audio tokens. That is INR 0.8 to 1.0 a minute against INR 2.6 to 3.4 on the current 3.1 model. It is Preview, and the price doubles on 1 Jan 2027, so it is still INR 1.6 to 2.0 a minute after that.
3) Documented Hinglish in Roman letters: Amazon Polly Aditi/Kajal (AWS doc) and Cartesia Sonic-3.6 (vendor blog). Sarvam Bulbul says Roman Hindi degrades quality and costs INR 2.70/min. Most others have no Roman-Hinglish claim, so listen to samples before choosing.
4) Only Polly states in writing that you may store and replay audio. Pre-generating fixed lessons once and caching them is the second lever.
5) The Gemini API (AI Studio) terms bar apps likely used by under-18s, and its free tier trains on your data. Vertex uses Cloud terms, where I found no such clause by text search.
CAVEATS: Not verified: I ran no synthesis and heard no audio, so quality, child-friendliness and Hinglish pronunciation are untested for every option. Hinglish claims are what vendors document, plus clearly labelled inference. Assumptions: INR 88 per USD, 900 characters (15 a second) per minute of speech. Characters were assumed to be counted as typed, with spaces.
