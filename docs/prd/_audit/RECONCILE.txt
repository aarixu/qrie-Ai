All figures use the measured 16.55 classes per student-hour (`last-class.json` activeMs 217,571 = 3.6262 min), prep at PRD 6's planning Rs 30/topic (so prep per student-hour = 16.55 x 30 / S = Rs 496.5 / S), and one denominator for every fixed line: the pilot's 2,500 student-hours/month (500 students x 5 h). Summing all six PRDs' fixed lines at that denominator: Supabase Pro Rs 2,390/mo = Rs 0.96/h (PRD 3); Piper VPS Rs 2,000/mo = Rs 0.80/h (PRD 6); Vercel Pro Rs 1,800/mo = Rs 0.72/h (PRD 2 — PRD 6's table omits it entirely, and PRD 2 divides it by 15,000 h to reach Rs 0.12, which is the main reason the six documents do not add up as written); R2 backups Rs 4/mo = Rs 0.0016/h (PRD 3). Fixed total Rs 2.48/student-hour.

END OF MVP-1. END OF MVP-2. This is where the six documents disagree with each other and PRD 6 disagrees with itself. WHERE IT BREAKS, PRECISELY. (1) MVP-1 and MVP-2 break on the judge, and only requirement 6/12's class-path playback change closes it; everything else across six documents is rounding against Rs 18.70. (2) Every MVP breaks at small S: at S=1 prep alone is Rs 496.5/student-hour, 99x the ceiling, and that is the default state of a self-serve student on a cold topic today, with no lock and no pre-warm. ######################################################################
RECONCILE SUMMARY
######################################################################
The six PRDs are individually strong and collectively not yet one document set. They agree on the spine's facts and on most measured numbers (16.55 classes/student-hour, Rs 164 today, Rs 30/topic prep, Rs 141/hr Piper saving), but they disagree on twelve substantive decisions, three of which decide whether the product clears Rs 5 at all.

The three that matter most. (1) The progress card is specified twice, incompatibly: PRD 4 builds it as a live RPC over a window, PRD 5 builds it as an immutable stored table with no authenticated read, and the two `ProgressCard` TypeScript types share a name and no fields. Three PRDs (1, 2, 6) consume it and would each get a different object. (2) The whyProbe class-path change — the only thing in the entire plan that takes the product under Rs 5 — is MVP-2 in PRD 4 and MVP-3 in PRD 6. That one staging disagreement is the difference between MVP-2 at Rs 3.49/student-hour and MVP-2 at Rs 22.19. PRD 6's own MVP-2 projection of Rs 8-12 is reachable under neither staging, which means PRD 6 contradicts itself. (3) `prepared_class` moderation is designed twice with different columns, different catalogue-read rules, different tools (`retire-class.mjs` vs `retract-class.mjs`) and different placements of the fact-check gate (PRD 3: asynchronous, server-side, after the class was taught; PRD 4: synchronous, inside `prepareClass`, before `saveable()`). PRD 6's pre-warm runner depends on PRD 3's version and calls it a hard non-negotiable gate.

Beneath those: the report payload has four incompatible definitions across four PRDs; the enrolment payload has three (`EnrollRequest`, `StudentWorkspace`, `StudentProfile`) with no carrier for `orgId`, `orgKind`, `source`, `campaign` or `board`, while PRD 3 makes `student_state.org_id` NOT NULL; PRD 6 depends on five schema objects (`usage_event.prep_id`, `usage_event.bucket`, `learning_session.prep_id`, `fixed_cost`, provider `'self'`) that PRD 3's migration set does not contain; and `PrewarmIntent` is produced by PRD 5, stored nowhere, and read by nobody.

The largest unowned item is not technical. PRD 0's decided contest-integrity requirements — recompute the prize metric server-side from `ledger_event`, run anomaly detection over the shortlist, bound `active_ms` against the wall clock — are assigned to PRD 3 and PRD 4 and appear in neither. A prize turns a client-written ledger into a target, and nothing in the six documents defends it. The second largest is PRD 0's language decision (English UI always, tutor language per student), which PRD 1 req 19 and PRD 5 req 1 both contradict, and whose real consequence — planning a class language-neutral and rendering narration per language — no PRD claims.

The critical path is not what the MVP labels imply. Four MVP-1 items have a hard ordering that no PRD states: `foldAnswer` before the fold; migrations 0014/0015 before the first real user closes the zero-row window; the whyProbe *format* before the pre-warm runner, or all 274 warmed classes must be re-prepared; and the global spend cap before the first deploy, not after it. PRD 3's default ceiling of Rs 500/day halts the pilot on day one (500 students x 5 h/month at Rs 25.4 is Rs 2,117/day) and halts a 5,000-student contest almost immediately against PRD 6's own Rs 125,000-750,000 estimate for it.
