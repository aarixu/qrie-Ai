== VERIFIED SUMMARY (post adversarial check) ==
Read from source. Curie has a real, working single-player LMS: an adaptive placement test (20 probes, packages/diagnostic/src/session.ts:44; seeded from classroom signals, Diagnostic.tsx:121) feeding a prerequisite-ordered plan capped at 12 steps (plan.ts:44,46), a lesson, a topic quiz, and quiz-driven plan adaptation (adaptPlan.ts:86, PracticeQuiz.tsx:196) — plus streak, ten achievements, doubt history, notes, bookmarks, per-subject progress and local reminders. It is computed on-device from localStorage and mirrored to one opaque jsonb table, student_state: twelve stores sync, and the thirteenth, the safety flag log, opts out and has its earlier cloud rows actively deleted (flags.ts:65, sync.ts:125-130). There is no second seat. A parent can see nothing: no route, screen or component addresses anyone but the student (App.tsx:343-380); no policy on any table holding a student's own record grants a read to another user; and no tool reads learning data with the service key (cost-report.mjs is spend; model-metric.mjs uses the student's own JWT). The schema does contain cross-user reads, just not of student data — is_member() on organization and membership (0005:124-131), and world-readable prepared classes, their public bucket and the price list (0004:45,60,68; 0008:109). Tenancy (0005) is one table pair with three roles, only 'student' ever written, and it is NOT unused as ARCHITECTURE.md:195 and docs/architecture/README.md:541 claim: every insert requires is_member(org_id) (0006:152,160) and every drain resolves an org first (ledgerSync.ts:229). membership.role is read, but only by two live-test tools. It cannot express a guardian, cohort, payer or section. DPDP machinery is zero — four prose comments, nothing else — while every install silently mints an auth user, an org and a membership at start-up.

== MISSING FOR MVP ==
  - Any DPDP / children's-data consent machinery at all. An exhaustive grep for consent, guardian, parental, DPDP, COPPA, age gate, date of birth, minor across every .ts/.tsx/.sql/.mjs in the tree returns three hits, all of them prose comments: 0005:7, 0007:3, telemetry.ts:62. No age or DOB is ever collected, no guardian is ever named, no consent is ever requested, recorded, versioned or withdrawable, and there is no consent table.
      Onboarding asks 'Which class are you in?' with options 6-12, which is the product asserting its users are roughly 11-18. India's DPDP Act requires verifiable parental consent before processing a child's personal data and separately bars behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children. The app collects the child's first name, class, subjects, free-text notes, every raised hand, every quiz answer and up to 200 characters of their own words per answer, and syncs all of it. This is the single largest legal blocker to Phase 4 and it is not partially built — it is absent.
  - A privacy policy, a terms acceptance, and a public account-deletion URL. There is no privacy document anywhere in the repo (find -iname '*privacy*' returns nothing), no terms link on SignIn.tsx or Onboarding.tsx, and no web-facing deletion page.
      Google Play requires a privacy policy URL, a Data Safety declaration, and — for a data-collecting app — a web-accessible deletion request URL in addition to the in-app one. The in-app deletion is real and good (0002); the web half does not exist. Play's Families policy adds more. The Android build cannot be submitted without these.
  - Any parent-facing artefact whatsoever: no second seat, no invite code, no read-only share link, no weekly summary by email or WhatsApp, no 'show a grown-up' screen, no spend visibility.
      0005's own text names the parent as the buyer in Indian edtech. The product currently gives the person paying literally nothing to look at. The cheapest honest version — a local, read-only 'this week' screen built from data already on the device (progress, streak, doubts, flags) — needs no backend, no tenancy and no consent change, and does not exist.
  - Any teacher- or school-facing artefact: no roster, no cohort/section/batch table, no enrolment, no assignment, no due date, no attendance, no gradebook, no CSV/PDF export, no report card, no bulk provisioning, no admin console.
      0005 says the LMS is 'a school or institute as admin, teachers under it, students under them, with homework and exam dates flowing into Curie'. None of the four nouns in that sentence — school, teacher-student link, homework, exam date — has a table, a type or a screen.
  - Any RLS pattern for 'an adult may read this child's rows'. Every select policy in every migration is `auth.uid() = user_id` (0001:24, 0006:148/156, 0008:105, 0013:110/114). There is not one example in the schema of a cross-user read.
      The claim that org_id on concept_state makes teacher visibility 'a policy change rather than a migration' is untested — no such policy has ever been written, and the tables that hold the data anyone would actually want to see (student_state above all) have no org_id to write it against.
  - A guardian relationship. membership can only say 'user X is in org O with role R'. It cannot say 'adult A is the guardian of child C', and the role check constraint (admin|teacher|student) has no value for parent or guardian.
      0005's stated first reason for existing is the DPDP question of 'this student belongs to that adult'. The schema it shipped cannot express that sentence. The constraint would have to be altered for exactly the role the migration was written for — the one thing it claims to have avoided.
  - A payer / billing entity. organization has id, kind, name, created_at and nothing else: no owner, no plan, no tier, no entitlement, no contact.
      'The parent usually pays' is 0005's second stated reason. There is nowhere to record who bought what for whom.
  - Retention for student_state. The 90-day purge (0007/0009) covers ledger_event and learning_session only. student_state — which holds the child's name, their full placement answers, their notes, their doubt log and their quiz history — has no expiry at all and lives until the account is deleted.
      The product makes a retention promise in a migration header about children's answers, and the table with the longest-lived, most identifying copy of those answers is outside it. Worse: student_state's policy is `for all`, so the student can also rewrite it — the ledger's append-only guarantee does not extend to the blob.
  - An audit trail of who read a child's record. No table, no log, no function.
      The moment a teacher or parent can read a student's rows, DPDP-grade practice needs a record of that access. Adding it after the second seat ships is the same retrofit 0005 was written to avoid.
  - Server-side queryable progress of any kind. The only cross-student server read in the repo is cost-report.mjs (spend, service key). model-metric.mjs reads a real student only through that student's own JWT, by design.
      Even with a consent flow and a teacher role, there is nothing to query: progress lives in localStorage and in an opaque jsonb blob keyed by localStorage key name. A parent view is blocked on the fold, not just on tenancy.
